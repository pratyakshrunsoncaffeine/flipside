**Scores**

- **Use of Switch: 8/10.** Addressing policy, author-only approval cards and two-owner rooms are all load-bearing, and a single CI bot could not copy them. The gate itself is just an ordinary bot.
- **Technical execution: 2/10.** Nothing is built, and K1 and K2 are the foundation, not side features.
- **Creativity and impact: 7/10.** It is the freshest angle in the crowded AI-review category, and the cross-team PR pain is real.
- **Presentation: 7/10.** It is unusually honest and clear, but there is no video, and the hero moment is a stock Switch message rather than something the project built.

Weighted total: 2.4 + 0.5 + 1.75 + 1.4 = **6.05 out of 10**.

**Shortlist: no.** It is the best-argued plan in its category, but judges score what runs, and today nothing does.

**Weaknesses, most costly first**

1. **Nothing has run.** Run K1 and K2 this week and commit the evidence: a screen recording and the server log. Then ship `make replay` and `make test` with green CI, so a judge can clone it and see the whole flow in under five minutes.
2. **"Verified" is self-attested.** The gate runs on the author's machine, and the reviewer can check neither the quote nor the hash. The hash is also recorded when the question arrives, so the author can edit the session after reading the questions. Fix:
   - Hash each step into a Merkle root and put the root in the PR description at PR open.
   - With each answer, disclose the step, its hash and the Merkle proof.
   - Let a gate owned by the reviewer check the proof.
   Then "verified" means something to the side that is actually asking.
3. **The impact evidence is thin and looks staged.** Twelve fixtures from one teammate, with 8 of the 12 rigged, is not a realistic base rate. Fix: use 30 or more PRs at a realistic rate of hidden changes, and have blind reviewers who are not the builders. Report minutes and hidden changes caught with confidence intervals, and state the base rate up front.

**Claims I doubt**

- **"The author approves the exact text."** A Claude Code permission prompt shows the command, which may be truncated. In `acceptEdits` or bypass mode it shows no card at all. The rest of the safety story depends on this.
- **"New agents default to `owner-agents`."** Cite the line and version. Judges from Switch will check it.
- **The question filter as a safety control.** The gate passes text written by the reviewer's LLM into the author's agent. That is a prompt-injection path, and a deterministic keyword filter will not hold against LLM-written text. The `.env` refusal in the demo comes from addressing, not from the filter, so the demo does not show the filter working. Show an injected question failing, or pass questions to the author's agent as structured data (file, line, CI fact) rather than free text.
- **The fixture metrics.** "Reviewer minutes" means little when the people timing it built the tool. Say who the reviewers were.
